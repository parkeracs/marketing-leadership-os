# April Dunford

Positioning consultant and former startup marketing executive, author of *Obviously Awesome* and *Sales Pitch*.

**Why I trust them:** Dunford spent about 25 years as a startup executive running marketing, product and sales, across seven B2B technology startups (most acquired, including Janna Systems by Siebel and DataMirror by IBM), and says she positioned, repositioned or launched 16 products. Since then she has worked as a positioning consultant with more than 200 companies, mostly growth-stage B2B tech. *Obviously Awesome* (2019, revised edition 2026) turned positioning from a fill-in-the-blanks statement into a repeatable, step-by-step method; her site reports over 100,000 copies sold. Much of the method is explained free on her blog, newsletter and podcast.

**Lens:** Positioning as context-setting, worked out from the customer's side. Start with what buyers would do if you didn't exist, then work through differentiated capabilities, value, best-fit customers and market category, in that order. Engineering-minded and process-driven, sceptical of category creation, and firmly focused on how a product wins deals today rather than on vision.

**Use for:** Choosing or fixing positioning, repositioning an existing product, picking a market category, and turning positioning into a sales pitch. See [[positioning-example]].

**Blind spots:**
- **B2B tech, sales-assisted, first.** The method and examples come from considered-purchase software. It says less about consumer, self-serve or community-led motions, where buyers rarely sit through a pitch.
- **Needs real customers.** The method works best once you have customers who love the product; she herself treats pre-launch positioning as a thesis to test.
- **Sceptical of new categories.** Category-design advocates disagree with her default to existing categories. Read both before deciding.
- **Light on brand and emotion.** Little on visual identity, voice or creative distinctiveness, which other sources treat as part of positioning in crowded markets.
- **Evidence is mostly her own experience.** Results such as Janna Systems' growth are first-hand accounts, not independent data.
