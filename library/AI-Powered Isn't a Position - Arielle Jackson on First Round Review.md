> [!summary] Principles
> - **A label is not a position.** "AI-powered X" stops differentiating as soon as several credible "AI-powered X" products exist. If buyers can't see the difference, they pick whatever is safest to justify internally.
> - **Start with an opinionated point of view:** what you believe that others don't. It should attract some buyers and repel others, and it outlasts any single release.
> - **Positioning is a set of coherent choices:** who you're for, why they should care, how their world is broken today, what you're up against, and how you differ from how they solve the problem now.
> - **Begin by positioning against the status quo** (manual work, legacy software, doing nothing), then sharpen against direct rivals as they appear. Gong first argued against "gut feel" coaching, and only later against Chorus.
> - **Brand is the visible expression of those choices,** across visual identity, language and product defaults. Aim to be distinctive but still recognisable.
> - **Two quick tests:** put your homepage hero, a customer email and a social post side by side, and check they share one POV. Then swap your logo for a competitor's: the page should stop making sense.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-05
> - In AI categories, positioning that once lasted a year or more needs revisiting every few months; founders often fight the status quo and several direct competitors at once.
> - Cursor launched as "AI-first" (October 2023), then sharpened against new AI editors, then had to reposition around agent orchestration once agent tools reframed the category.
> - Clay is cited as a brand whose distinctive assets (bright rainbow logo, billboard campaign) make imitation look like parody.
