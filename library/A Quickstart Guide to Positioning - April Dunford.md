> [!summary] Principles
> - **Positioning is context-setting.** The category you name triggers buyer assumptions about competitors, features, buyer and price. Good positioning triggers true assumptions; bad positioning leaves sales to undo the damage.
> - **Positioning is not messaging,** a tagline, a brand story or a vision. Those come after it.
> - **Five components, worked in order:** competitive alternatives → differentiated capabilities → value → best-fit customers → market category. Each depends on the one before, so the order matters.
> - **Start with what customers would do if you didn't exist,** not a list of every possible competitor. The status quo (a spreadsheet, a manual process, an intern) is often the main rival.
> - **Ignore "phantom competitors"** you never meet in deals; positioning against them waters you down.
> - **The fill-in-the-blanks positioning statement is dangerous:** it assumes one right answer per blank, when most products could win in several markets.
> - **Default to an existing category, narrowed.** "CRM for investment banks" borrows what buyers already understand. Creating a new category is slow and expensive, and creators often lose to later entrants.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2021-03
> - Enterprise software typically loses about 25% of deals to "no decision", in the author's experience.
> - About 90% of tech companies that went public in the previous five years positioned in existing categories (no source given).
> - Janna Systems grew from under $2M to close to $80M in about 18 months after repositioning from "enterprise CRM" to "CRM for investment banks" (author's own account of a past role).

## Notes

- **Origin story:** a "Microsoft Access killer" that had barely sold. Of 100 buyers called, 94 didn't use it and 6 had transformed field sales or service with it. Repositioned as an embeddable database for mobile devices, it took off.
- **Why order matters:** starting anywhere else produced positioning that "sounded good in the office" but wasn't differentiated. Jobs To Be Done thinking pointed to alternatives first.
- **Janna Systems:** facing Siebel, the team described its unique feature's value as "anything you want." An investment-bank customer revealed the real value (relationship insight), which defined the segment, then the narrower category.
