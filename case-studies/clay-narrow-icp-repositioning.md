# Clay: narrowing a horizontal tool to one ICP

> [!summary] At a glance
> Clay spent years as a general-purpose "spreadsheet connected to APIs" that recruiters, engineers and salespeople all used a little. Growth came only after the team committed to one use case, outbound sales, and then an even narrower wedge: data enrichment for cold-email agencies. Nearly all existing customers churned *(claimed)* [3]. Clay then grew revenue 10x in 2022, 10x in 2023 and 6x in 2024 *(claimed)* [4], reaching a $1.25B valuation in January 2025 *(verified)* [4][5].

## Who built it

Kareem Amin co-founded Clay in 2017 [2]. Varun Anand joined in 2021 and led the go-to-market work that followed, later becoming co-founder [3].

## How it worked

1. **A broad product with too many ICPs.** The early product pulled data from the web into spreadsheet cells. Recruiters, salespeople, front-end engineers and even an accounting use case all found it useful, and the team kept chasing each one [1][2].
2. **The spiral.** Amin describes deciding to target salespeople, getting sidetracked by a promising adjacent request (inbound enrichment, an API, a CRM, recruiters), then coming back to the same decision [1, 00:08:00–00:13:00].
3. **Commitment that showed.** About three and a half years in, the team was focused on salespeople internally but not in the product. The real shift came when they cut features, rewrote the website and documentation for salespeople only, and made sure nobody described Clay in general terms [1, 00:09:00][2].
4. **One rule for sales:** sell the same thing every time. If a prospect wanted something else, they weren't the right customer yet [1, 00:14:00].
5. **An even narrower wedge.** Anand searched a sales community's archive for people discussing enrichment and outbound, and interviewed about 30. Cold-email agency owners felt the pain most: many clients with the same need, technical, scrappy and price-sensitive [3].
6. **Go where the wedge already talks.** The team joined agency WhatsApp and Slack groups, hired a well-known agency owner as an in-house expert, and ran "reverse demos" in which prospects drove the product on their own data [3].

## Why it worked

Our reading, not the founders': narrowing turned the five positioning components into clear answers: one alternative (duct-taped outbound tools), one valuable difference (many data sources in one place), one segment that cared most. That made the message, the community and the product roadmap point the same way. Amin's own summary is that narrowing the scope increased the product's value [2].

## When it transfers, and when it doesn't

- **Transfers** when a flexible product has scattered, lukewarm usage across many segments, and one segment shows outsized pull.
- **Doesn't** when you have no segment with acute pain, or can't afford to lose your current customers while you rebuild.
- Clay's wedge (agencies) was too small to be the end state [3]. Plan the next steps out, as Dunford's "CRM for investment banks, then financial services" did ([[A Quickstart Guide to Positioning - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
- The later results also reflect AI timing and community-led growth, so not all of them are down to positioning.

## How to run your own

- [ ] List every segment using the product, and rank them by pain and pull
- [ ] Pick one, and write down what you will stop doing
- [ ] Cut features, website copy and docs that serve other segments
- [ ] Sell the same thing every time; say no to off-wedge deals
- [ ] Find where that segment already talks, and show up there
- [ ] Decide which segment comes next, and what will earn you the right to expand
